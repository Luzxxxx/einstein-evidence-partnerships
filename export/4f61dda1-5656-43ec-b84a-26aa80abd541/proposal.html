C036 / R0976

Challenge C036-CH1 · approved draft version 1

summary

Dr. Jelena Marković (R0976) is a strong partial match for C036-CH1: her attributed work covers raspberry MAP gas composition for Serbian export, film OTR/CO₂TR screening with permeation modelling, pre-cooling effects on Botrytis, and temperature-driven shelf-life prediction on real Serbian consignments. This addresses the modelling gap and the film-specification translation the company lacks, but it does not by itself guarantee <1% mould at day 14; a bounded trial is the appropriate next step.

Claims: C1, C3, C7, C8

technicalRationale

Raspberry-specific evidence: a 5% O₂/15% CO₂ atmosphere cut Botrytis incidence 63% versus air and held firmness for 12 days at 2 °C, giving a defensible starting point for atmosphere targets.

Claims: C1, C2

Film specification capability: 12-film OTR/CO₂TR screening with finite-element gas-permeation modelling shows a method to convert measured permeability and headspace data into a candidate specification, matching the company's stated need to translate OTR/CO₂TR numbers into a film grade.

Claims: C3, C6

Condensation and pre-cooling: evidence on forced-air versus room cooling and hydrocooling links equilibration speed and free moisture to Botrytis incidence, relevant to the company's micro-condensation hypothesis.

Claims: C4

Cold-chain data use: existing work ingesting temperature/humidity streams into Botrytis-relevant prediction models indicates usable methodology for FreshPak's two seasons of logs and destination inspection reports.

Claims: C5

cooperationScope

Scope: a film-specification and trial-design project using FreshPak's lab (OTR/CO₂TR, headspace gas, seal strength) and historic shipment data, with milestones (a) translation of logs into a temperature/humidity profile, (b) modelled headspace and Botrytis risk ranking for a short candidate film list, (c) simulated-transit trial protocol and its execution, (d) pre-season consignment check.

Claims: C9, C10

Engagement form to agree: joint project and/or industrial PhD co-supervision, consistent with the researcher's stated openness.

Claims: C11

validation

Baseline: current lidding film and seal pressure run in the simulated-transit protocol, recording visible Botrytis incidence at days 9 and 14 and headspace O₂/CO₂. Candidate films are measured the same way, with model-predicted versus observed equilibrium gas composition compared per film and fill weight. Go/no-go: advance to the pre-season consignment check only if the best candidate reaches the agreed mould-incidence threshold and its observed headspace trend is consistent with the model; otherwise revise film, seal setting or pre-cooling before the season deadline.

Claims: C10, C2, C3, C4

constraints

Required inputs from FreshPak: the two current film grades and their measured OTR/CO₂TR, standard punnet geometry and fill weight, seal pressure range, two seasons of temperature/humidity logs and destination inspection reports, and lab time for headspace and seal testing. Engagement is limited to the researcher's lab and part-time involvement; no large external testing programme can be assumed given the stated budget.

Claims: C9, C11

What remains outside the supported scope: heat-seal and anti-fog film behaviour, micro-condensation control in the 125 g rPET shell, compostable-material migration, and full commercial deployment or a contractual one-percent mould guarantee.

Claims: C7, C8

unknowns

Open questions: which factor dominates visible mould (field inoculum, pre-cooling delay, condensation, atmosphere imbalance); how anti-fog chemistry and seal integrity interact with condensation; whether simulated transit reproduces German/Netherlands destination outcomes; and whether any candidate film can reach <1% at day 14 within budget.

Claims: C12, C8

Claims, evidence and limitations

C1 (fact)

Peer-reviewed work attributed to this researcher evaluated MAP of Serbian-grown raspberries at 2 °C over 14 days across three gas compositions, monitoring firmness, colour, soluble solids, titratable acidity and visual mould incidence plus Botrytis cinerea counts.

  • Results are at lab/experimental scale under controlled 2 °C storage, not on commercial export consignments.
  • Abstract-level report only; film OTR/CO₂TR of the tested films is not stated in the retrieved excerpt.

C2 (fact)

The reported raspberry MAP study found that a 5% O₂/15% CO₂ atmosphere reduced Botrytis incidence by 63% relative to an ambient-air control and maintained firmness above commercial thresholds for 12 days versus 6 days in air, with sensory marketability at day 10 under optimal MAP.

  • 63% reduction is relative to control, so it does not imply sub-1% absolute mould incidence by day 14.
  • Sensor panel marketability at day 10 falls short of the company's 14-day target.

C3 (fact)

Related work attributed to this researcher screened 12 commercial films differing in OTR and CO₂TR for mixed-berry trays at 2 °C and used finite-element gas-permeation modelling to confirm headspace trends and predict equilibrium under alternative fill weights; one film (OTR 3,800, CO₂TR 18,000 cm³/m²/day) kept headspace within acceptable ranges for three species and cut mould incidence 55% versus a polyethylene control.

  • The tray was mixed-berry, not raspberry-only 125 g rPET punnet geometry.
  • Film specification values are reported as study findings and cannot be transferred to the company's punnet without local verification.

C4 (fact)

The researcher's raspberry MAP study also examined pre-cooling before sealing: forced-air cooling reached target core temperature in 38 minutes versus 210 minutes for room cooling, gave 14 hours earlier headspace O₂ equilibration, and was linked to significantly lower Botrytis incidence and better firmness at day 8; hydrocooling produced excess free moisture and calyx decay.

  • Findings concern pre-cooling protocols at 2 °C experimental scale; applicability to the company's packing-house line is unverified.
  • Day-8 outcomes do not demonstrate day-14 performance.

C5 (fact)

Attributed work translates cold-chain data into shelf-life prediction: a 12-month digital-twin pilot on Serbian berry consignments flagged 88% of consignments later showing elevated spoilage with 9% false positives, and a Monte Carlo stochastic model propagating temperature variability from 180 monitored consignments covered 91% of observed outcomes, identifying mean transport temperature and door-opening frequency as the dominant variance drivers.

  • The stochastic framework is demonstrated on strawberries and total aerobic count; Botrytis-specific calibration on raspberries is separate.
  • Modelling addresses prediction and prioritisation, not film selection by itself.
