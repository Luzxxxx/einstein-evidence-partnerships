C025 / R0637

Challenge C025-CH1 · approved draft version 1

summary

R0637 is the best-supported candidate: her recorded work spans automated Gerber-based emission-risk rule checking, near-field-scan triage versus accredited OATS results, PDN impedance control and return-path discontinuity modelling, which maps onto Nexara's need to catch 150–450 MHz emission hotspots at layout review rather than after lab failure.

Claims: C1, C2, C3, C4, C7

The fit is partial and unverified on Nexara's hardware; the pilot is designed to test whether those methods actually discriminate Nexara's passing and failing boards before any checklist is released.

Claims: C7, C8, C11

technicalRationale

The rule-checker provides an early layout gate on Gerber/netlist geometry, while the published workflow adds near-field scanning and FDTD quantification only for flagged hotspots, matching Nexara's stated preference for a self-run workflow over per-board consultancy.

Claims: C2, C3, C7

PDN impedance and ground-plane moat findings give concrete physical mechanisms and candidate checks for the frequency band of interest, though each was validated on non-Nexara vehicles.

Claims: C5, C6, C4

cooperationScope

Contribution: retrospective triage of Nexara's five boards with the open rule-checker, PDN and return-path checks, targeted near-field scanning of flagged hotspots, and delivery of a documented layout checklist and simulation workflow with a worked example per failed board.

Claims: C8, C3, C5, C6

Milestones (proposed, to confirm): data handover under NDA; baseline reproducibility check; retrospective scoring and hotspot analysis on the three failed boards; checklist draft reviewed with Nexara engineers; final workflow handover and training session.

Claims: C8, C10

validation

Baseline: Nexara's current HyperLynx impedance checks plus in-house pre-compliance scans and the recorded 3-of-5 first-submission failure history. Measurements: per-board rule score versus known pass/fail, hotspot location agreement with pre-compliance/near-field data, and PDN impedance and moat-penalty indicators on flagged regions.

Claims: C9, C7

Proposed go/no-go: release the checklist to Nexara engineers only if retrospective triage flags the known failures with a false-positive rate agreed in advance; otherwise revise or abandon. Track subsequent first-submission outcomes as the real test, without promising accredited-lab success.

Claims: C9, C11

constraints

Required inputs: Gerber files, BOM/netlist and pre-compliance scan data for the five boards, plus the target class limits and frequency band, all under NDA.

Claims: C10, C8

Participation conditions: work framed as contract research/EMC design-review collaboration; scanning bench available only on cost-recovery terms when teaching commitments permit; budget, lab hours and schedule are not established and must be agreed.

Claims: C10, C4

unknowns

Open questions: which mechanisms dominate the three failures; whether HyperLynx workflows at Nexara can be reconfigured for emissions prediction or a separate FDTD route is needed; whether R0637's bench access and doctoral supervision load permit near-field scanning; and whether results transfer to the Budapest lab's class limits.

Claims: C11, C7, C10

C2 (fact)

R0637 co-authored work on a rule-based PCB layout scoring tool that ingests Gerber files and a netlist, flags geometric emission-risk violations under 17 rules, and was validated on 23 boards of known emission performance with 83% sensitivity and 22% false-positive rate; the authors describe it as an early design gate, not a replacement for simulation or pre-compliance measurement.

  • Validation was against CISPR 22 Class B results on 23 boards; not verified for Nexara's board types, frequency range or class limits.
  • Reported sensitivity/false-positive figures come from that study only and are not guaranteed to transfer.

C3 (fact)

R0637 co-authored an integrated pre-compliance workflow that sequences layout rule checking, near-field scanning at 3 mm standoff and FDTD simulation; across four prototype boards with two regional companies it identified the dominant emission source in each case, and corrective measures reduced peak radiated emission by 5.2–11.7 dB with 2.1 fewer test-and-rework cycles versus a historical baseline.

  • Four-board sample developed with two partner companies; not Nexara's designs or Budapest lab.
  • Improvements were measured at the pre-compliance receiver, not formal OATS certification outcomes.

C4 (fact)

R0637 co-authored a study showing near-field scan data at 3 mm standoff gave 85% rank-ordering agreement with accredited 3 m OATS results at each board's emission maximum, with absolute amplitude within ±4.5 dB in 74% of cases, degrading to ±9 dB above 700 MHz; the authors frame it as early design triage, not a certification substitute.

  • Seven consumer electronics boards; standoff and grid parameters matter and are not guaranteed to apply to Nexara's boards.
  • Accuracy stated is not a guarantee of passing accredited measurement.

C6 (fact)

C7 (inference)

Taken together, R0637's evidence supports building Nexara a repeatable pre-signoff emission-risk triage: an automated Gerber/netlist rule-score gate plus PDN and return-path checks, escalated to near-field scanning and FDTD only for flagged hotspots. That is a plausible route to catching hotspots during layout review, but the published accuracies are study-specific and the 150–450 MHz band and Nexara's class limits would need re-validation on Nexara's own five boards.

  • None of the published validation used Nexara hardware or the Budapest accredited lab; transferability is unverified.
  • Evidence does not establish that any single tool explains all three failures.

C8 (proposal)

Proposed bounded pilot: (1) under NDA, retrospectively run the open rule-checker on Nexara's five boards' Gerber files and netlists, comparing scores against known pass/fail and pre-compliance scan data; (2) for the three failed boards, apply PDN-impedance and return-path/moat checks to the flagged regions; (3) near-field scan only the identified hotspots at the faculty bench where access permits; (4) distill the discriminative checks into a documented layout checklist and a step-by-step simulation workflow for Nexara engineers to run themselves.

  • Retrospective scoring on five boards is a small sample and may not be statistically conclusive.
  • Scanning bench access is cost-recovery and subject to faculty teaching commitments; timing cannot be promised.
  • If retrospective triage does not separate failing from passing boards, the checklist must be revised or the pilot stopped.

  • Pass criteria must not be framed as guaranteeing accredited-lab success.

C11 (inference)

Boundaries: the evidence covers radiated-emission prediction, PDN impedance, return-path discontinuities and near-field triage, which aligns with the 150–450 MHz failure mode, but it does not cover full HyperLynx-based emissions flow configuration, accredited CISPR class-limit sign-off, or every failure mechanism (e.g. cable/harness and enclosure effects) that can drive Budapest failures. Establishing that a checklist reduces re-test cost and delay is a prospective claim that must be verified within the company's own process.

  • No evidence in this bundle about EMC labs, CISPR classes or failure mechanisms specific to Nexara.
  • Remaining requirements outside the tool's scope must be identified with Nexara during the pilot.
